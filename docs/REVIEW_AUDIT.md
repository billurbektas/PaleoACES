Review date: 2026-10-06.

The most consequential concerns are scientific interpretation and provenance: Panther Potholes' primary chronology deliberately retains five inherited activity uncertainties in the age-error field to reproduce the published chronology; tephra bracket widths do not establish statistical age errors; the plotted two-axis CCA explains 15.34% of total inertia; and some descriptive groups contain only two units. The ²¹⁰Pb issue and the assumed-error sensitivity were already documented before this audit; the review confirms that known limitation and preserves the existing reproduction decision. Remaining methodological questions are recorded for the author. No chronology was refitted and no scientific settings were changed.

Confirmed technical corrections address nested script environments, missing CCA constraint metadata, omitted report summaries, invalid climate values/coordinates, and coverage gaps that could be bridged into continuous eras or fire periods. The 522 independent numerical checks reproduce current CCA inertia, tracking, direction, climate aggregation, and fire arithmetic. Validation evidence and exact before/after effects are recorded below.

## Inputs, vegetation, and taxonomy

The active producers use the original MORA legacy workbook, MORA cleaned tree/seedling resurveys, NOCA's original legacy workbook, and the received 2026 corrected NOCA tree workbook. The NOCA patch reconciles 2,366 stems in 54 corrected plots against the original erroneous source groups before replacing them. The cleaned NOCA universe contains 307 plots; `BAK487` and `BEAV6061` are explicit study exclusions. A cleaning universe is distinct from calibration or valid projection membership.

Presence is adult **or** regeneration presence, collapsed once per site, period, plot, and taxon. Original MORA metadata supports separating size-class tree counts from bare-column understory cover. NOCA's TREE and SHRUB worksheets support the stated stratum distinction. Regeneration bands differ: NOCA legacy uses 0–3 m height, whereas resurveys use 10–140 cm seedlings. Adult limits also differ: above 3 m in the legacy NOCA survey versus above 1.4 m in the resurvey. The stage-count caption's former assertion that adults were comparable across all panels was therefore too strong and has been corrected. Neither stratum is a uniform size-class measurement across periods. No band, exclusion, species name, or presence rule was changed.

Independent set counts reproduce the threshold-20 retained set of 13 taxa and 620 legacy calibration rows, with unique binary consolidated keys. Modern valid tracking contains 330 MORA and 277 NOCA pairs. Selection uses counts across all site-period groups, including resurvey and paleo groups: projection samples do not fit the CCA, but they influence the retained-taxon rule. This is a defined analytical choice, not an independently validated out-of-sample predictive design.

The CARI449/CARI450 coordinate collision remains unresolved. Sign normalization maps both to the same latitude/longitude despite elevations of 622 and 1,149 m. Climate extraction therefore assigns the same grid cell to two differently labelled elevations. Neither coordinates nor plot inclusion were altered; authoritative source confirmation is required.

## Chronology

The seven MORA radiocarbon ages/errors were checked against Dunwiddie (1986), Table 1; coded depths are sample-range midpoints. Walsh (2017), Table 2, corroborates the MORA tephra point ages 470, 2,250, 3,650, and 5,740 cal BP. Calendar-year conversion uses `1950 - year_CE`; Crandell's years before 1970 require subtracting 20 to express cal BP. The source conventions support the existing calibration flags; already-calendar ages must not be calibrated again. These controls and the adopted settings remain unchanged. [Dunwiddie (1986)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1938503), [Walsh et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2017.10.032), and the colocated [control register](../Cores/CONTROL_POINTS.md) document the source trail.

**Previously discussed units limitation, deliberately retained for reproduction:** Prichard (2009), Table 1c, states that the errors 3.47, 1.15, 0.72, 0.74, and 0.77 are in DPM/g. They are activity errors, not years. This was already identified in the [NOCA paleo source notes](../data/NOCA/NOCA_paleo/SOURCES.md), the [control-point register](../Cores/CONTROL_POINTS.md), and the chronology script's sensitivity discussion. The earlier decision was to reproduce Gavin's primary chronology unchanged and retain a separately labelled, assumed ±10-year sensitivity. The source notes also record earlier proportional and ±15/±20-year comparisons and the rationale for keeping a constant assumption. The audit did not discover an unnoticed new programming error or supersede that decision. It confirms the dimensional limitation: the assumed sensitivity does not recover laboratory CRS age uncertainty or validate the primary error units. The [scientific-decision summary](SCIENTIFIC_DECISIONS.md) now makes this history explicit. No new sensitivity or refit was performed. [Prichard et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yqres.2009.03.008).

The existing sensitivity figure's subtitle says the models are identical below 35 cm. A comparison of the **stored** tables finds maximum differences below that depth of 15 years in the median, 24 years in the lower 95% bound, and 28 years in the upper bound. The [stored-sensitivity comparison](review/PB210_STORED_SENSITIVITY_COMPARISON.csv) records this evidence. The difference is modest relative to deeper uncertainty, but “identical” is inaccurate. The protected chronology script and stored fit remain unchanged; correcting that subtitle requires an explicit figure-only provenance revision.

**Uncertainty interpretation needs correction:** a half-range of calibrated brackets is not, by itself, a demonstrated one-standard-deviation uncertainty; calibration of a bracket's central ages does not propagate its full uncertainty distribution. In particular, revised Mount St Helens date errors do not establish tighter errors for Mount Rainier layers C/F. Statements in the chronology provenance documents that certain assumed errors are “correct” or “harmless” exceed the available evidence. Preserve the current values while deciding how to describe or revise their uncertainty. Bacon's date model uses its stated error parameter with a Student-t likelihood by default; it does not convert an arbitrary bracket width into a justified probability distribution. [Official rbacon manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/rbacon/rbacon.pdf).

Stored whole-chronology rows are sampled, preserving within-core depth dependence; duplicate median-age sample IDs are collapsed consistently. Reporting redraws only the existing figure section from stored posteriors. All 28 protected chronology files were unchanged in the baseline rebuild. The original MORA coring-year confirmation and corrected NOCA depths are traceable to the supplied provenance records, but the original correspondence and laboratory CRS age-error calculation are unavailable. Those claims remain partly unverified. Single-chain mixing diagnostics and 100 selected posterior rows do not establish uncertainty in paleoclimate forcing, taxonomy, or the regional charcoal records.

The wider fingerprint audit found that `Bacon(run=FALSE)` rewrites the `Ran on` timestamp in four serialized `.bacon` files. Their remaining contents were identical, and no MCMC ran. Posterior-read and report-redraw guards now restore these configurations as well. The final rebuild verifies all 28 control/posterior/age files plus seven serialized configuration files, including existing stored sensitivities, rather than silently treating a read-time timestamp as a chronology generation date.

## Climate preparation

Official CHELSA documentation identifies temperature in Kelvin and monthly precipitation mass per area, numerically equivalent to millimetres of water. A direct read of one official V2.1 monthly raster confirmed a GDAL scale of 0.1, zero offset, and a decoded value of 261.6 K at Panther Potholes. `terra` applies this metadata before extraction; subtracting 273.15 is appropriate, and manually multiplying again would be wrong. A local raster test also confirmed that extraction with the actual data-frame coordinates includes an ID column, making the current value-column selection valid. [CHELSA monthly dataset](https://www.chelsa-climate.org/datasets/chelsa_monthly), [file specification](https://os.zhdk.cloud.switch.ch/envidat-doi/10.16904_envidat.228/chelsa_file_specification.pdf), and [terra extraction documentation](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/terra/html/extract.html).

Independent monthly aggregation reproduces every current spring/winter temperature and summer/winter precipitation value used in modern calibration/projection. It checks common-variable month alignment, complete three-month seasons, and modern December's assignment to the following winter. Paleo December stays within its native climatology slice. Native matching uses existing available slices and the stated first-minimum tie rule. Temperature `(tasmin + tasmax)/2` is a midpoint proxy, not necessarily the mean of hourly temperatures; documentation now describes that distinction without changing the calculation.

Modern contrasts remain 1979–1989 versus 2010–2019 at MORA (30.5 midpoint years) and versus 2010–2015 at NOCA (28.5 years). Vegetation elapsed times use resurvey years separately. MORA's legacy survey-year provenance and possible CHELSA precipitation discontinuity remain unresolved. Raw means across unequal modern windows are annualized contrasts, not measured instantaneous trends. The modern model's within-variable extrapolation counts are descriptive checks only; falling inside each marginal calibration range does not demonstrate support within the joint multivariate calibration domain.

Cache validation now rejects Inf/NaN values, missing metadata, duplicate keys, absent months, and inconsistent coordinates across rows for the same ID. Monthly derivation also rejects invalid values and duplicate keys instead of allowing a two-month precipitation sum to masquerade as a complete season. The documented January 1979 temperature gap remains supported. Plain `--offline` still intentionally consumes existing aggregate climate products; it does not validate native-cache/input changes. Use `--offline --report` for the audited cache-validated rebuild. Historical remote raster contents cannot be reconstructed solely from cached CSVs; recorded cache hashes establish the reviewed local data, not an immutable historical server release.

## CCA and geometric interpretation

An independent correspondence-analysis residual matrix was projected onto the four weighted environmental constraints using base-R QR decomposition and SVD. Total inertia and all four constrained eigenvalues agree with `vegan` to floating-point precision. The current fit has total inertia 3.040468688 and constrained inertia 0.501167165 (16.48%). CCA1 contributes 9.30% and CCA2 6.05% of total inertia: the displayed plane represents 15.34% of total inertia, approximately 93.08% of constrained inertia. The remaining community variation is not represented in the two-axis tracking metric.

Binary presence inputs still yield CCA's chi-square row/column weighting; plots and taxa are not equally weighted merely because presence is binary. LC positions are linear combinations of constraints; WA positions derive from species composition using the fitted species weights and score normalization. These are descriptive ordination quantities, not validated forecasts or direct abundance measurements. The current scaling-1, Hill-off geometry is preserved. [Official vegan prediction documentation](https://vegandevs.github.io/vegan/reference/predict.cca.html).

Translation, rotation, reflection, and common scaling behave as expected in independent known examples. Unequal axis scaling changes Euclidean lengths/angles; the prior scaling diagnostic's assertion that species directions stay identical under unequal axis scaling was incorrect and its caption was repaired. Within-axis rank and sign can remain unchanged while two-axis angles change. The climate-correlation diagnostic now wraps its previously clipped footer and qualifies the former claim that a sign reversal necessarily breaks model transfer: marginal covariance changes alone do not establish predictive validity. Inertia and coordinates quoted in historical code comments are now explicitly labelled historical.

Figure 3's NOCA legacy mean uses 290 positioned plots and its resurvey mean 277. It therefore combines movement with endpoint membership differences. Figure 4/5's plotwise metrics instead use the 277 valid paired NOCA plots. The analysis-review wording now calls Figure 3's arrows site means; changing the centroid membership would be a scientific display decision and was not done. Correlated climate constraints make marginal inertia losses nonadditive; species contributions and variable-deletion contributions have different interpretations.

## Climate eras and fire

Independent calculations reproduce signed regional phase means, median phase scores, the eight-of-ten directional support criterion, variable-specific 50%-of-own-maximum thresholds, and the union of qualifying variables. Because a fixed positive SD multiplies every value and its own maximum equally, that SD cancels from this threshold decision. It still affects displayed standardized magnitudes and combined multivariate rates. No alternative standardization or threshold was run.

The eight current CC/S boundaries remain as recorded in the [scientific decisions](SCIENTIFIC_DECISIONS.md#unified-1000-year-climate-eras). Missing coverage is not relative stability. The corrected run-grouping logic splits otherwise-identical labels across uncovered grid cells. An analytic example previously turned two supported 100-year cells separated by a missing cell into one 300-year era; it now returns two 100-year eras. A separate synthetic MORA fire example likewise verifies that a one-core coverage gap cannot connect two high-fire periods. Current complete-coverage classifications are unchanged.

Accepted-event counts remain 17/14/13 for Sunrise/Shadow/Little Sunrise and 66 for Panther Potholes. Every current fire window was independently recounted using `[t-500, t+500)`; core normalization and site median plus `1.4826 × MAD` were independently recomputed. Frequency normalization emphasizes a core's relative activity rather than common absolute detection rates. Interpolation resolution, smoothing, thresholds, record chronology, and spatial support differ among cores. No detector setting was tuned.

Modern mapped fire years do not postdate the resurvey year for any current modeled pair. However, the MORA disturbance product covers 1987–2017, leaving part of the interval after the operational 1977.5 legacy date unobserved. “Unburned” therefore means no mapped fire evidence under that product's coverage, rather than demonstrated absence throughout the entire survey interval. Holocene fire categories use fixed median event chronologies; event-timing uncertainty is not propagated into those category assignments.

Official CharAnalysis R 2.0.3 reproduces 15/16/14 episodes, whereas Walsh reports 24/18/16. This is an unresolved software/publication discrepancy, distinct from the primary tapas catalog. Prichard's original noise-distribution description differs from the harmonized Gaussian-mixture detector; retaining its interpolation/percentile choices does not mean the modern implementation exactly reproduces every published algorithmic detail. Neither matching a published total nor tuning to force agreement is evidence of correct local event timing. [Official CharAnalysis documentation](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/CharAnalysis/CharAnalysis.pdf), [Walsh et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2017.10.032), [Prichard et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yqres.2009.03.008).

## Tracking, direction, and summaries

Every current modern row and both existing Holocene resolutions were recalculated independently from their stored endpoints. Checks include displacement, rate with separate elapsed times, magnitude/rate ratios, cosine/angle, along-trajectory projection, perpendicular movement using a cross-product formula, endpoint lag, signed lag change, trajectory error, and all signed physical climate changes/rates. The primary run has 15,270 realizations; all 607 modern pairs remain in the numerical tracking stage. These checks found no demonstrated arithmetic error in the current finite endpoints.

All 10,319 modern and 259,590 primary Holocene direction contrasts were independently matched to their source movements and raw targets. The own-origin formulas and their NA masks agree with the implementation. A difference of zero means equal target cosines, not equal vectors; opposite-side or geometrically different trajectories can have equal target alignment. The climate arrows are common directions; species targets are points relative to separate LC/WA origins. The estimand is therefore target alignment under the chosen biplot geometry, not directly species gain, dominance, or causal climate response.

Chronology draws summarize within each offset before complete-offset site-date summaries; group summaries are descriptive. The 137 supported site-dates overlap in their temporal support. Posterior rows, offsets, overlapping dates, and taxa do not supply independent ecological replicates. MORA burned has two plots, and NOCA stable/high-fire has two dates. There are 153 stationary modern compositions with undefined directions. No significance or causal conclusion is justified by these descriptive group counts alone. Group medians of nonlinear metrics are not the metric of a group-median trajectory.

## Programming, reports, and corrections

Nested `source()` calls previously used R's default global environment despite the driver's separate stage environments. This lost local caller settings and left the exported CCA constraint list empty. Shared definitions now load into their caller environment; the protected age-depth script and Bacon settings remain byte-identical to the checkpoint. `rbacon`'s required global `info` exchange remains explicitly contained by the existing chronology-report wrapper. Package attachment, options, and RNG state still belong to the R process; object isolation does not imply a completely hermetic process. [Official R `source()` documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/base/html/source.html).

The report builder selected retired metric identifiers, silently leaving only angle rows in two inline summaries even though the linked CSVs contained lag and tracking ratio. It now selects `lag_rate_per_century`, `rate_tracking_ratio`, and `angle_degrees`, and validates their presence together with the fitted constraints. Scientific CSV values are unchanged by this presentation repair.

The turnover helper also failed when a sample contained only persistent, lost, or gained taxa: the absent change classes did not become columns before arithmetic. They now receive zero counts; analytically known all-persistent and one-period examples pass. Current datasets already contain every change class, so their values remain unchanged.

The original staged whitespace check was incomplete: untracked generated CSVs use CRLF, inherited files contain whitespace, and Markdown intentionally uses trailing spaces for line breaks. This is not a scientific failure. The checkpoint preserves those bytes; this audit does not claim a clean all-file whitespace check. Active code parses and executes, and validation captures command exit status separately.

Several optional diagnostics retain comments about former CHELSAcruts splicing, 500-year bins, or anchored climate. They are not current-method authorities. When the active download disables bias correction, its stored `value_corrected` field mirrors `value_raw`; that alone is not an independently rebuilt anchoring sensitivity. The current report uses the climate diagnostic's correlation page and explicitly labels its other pages as the existing 500-year diagnostic framework.

Independent current-method checks, focused edge-case regressions, corrected full rebuilds, fresh-session versus sequential-stage comparisons, missing/invalid-cache failures, stale-report rejection, deliberate report-product deletion/regeneration, and a portable Git export are detailed in the [validation record](review/VALIDATION_RESULTS.json).

Zero scientific values changed. The four main PNGs are byte-identical to the checkpoint; changed diagnostic previews contain caption repairs and a worked-example legend layout change, without changing the plotted scientific values. All 1,262 protected input/cache/chronology files, including all 51 files under `Cores/`, match the checkpoint in both original and isolated copies; the two relocated-source documentation companions are the only explicit nonresearch exceptions. Paths, source-code fingerprints, cache dependency metadata, and producer ledgers are provenance fields and are explicitly identified; they are not silently excluded as unspecified differences. All current CCA settings, controls/posteriors, era boundaries, accepted fire events, and tracking/direction calculations remain fixed. Technical fixes affect invalid-input behavior, uncovered synthetic intervals, configuration ownership, and displayed report content. Four fresh-session/sequential-order cases reproduce the same 113 CSV, gzipped, and RDS files without leaking the model or suffix into the global environment. Eight restored failure probes and six existing offline guard probes reject invalid or missing caches and stale report evidence, and preserve published products. An injected posterior-read error also verifies configuration restoration. Deliberately removed scaling preview, fitted-CCA summary, and age-draw cache were regenerated successfully. The reconstructed temporary checkout regenerates age draws from the same protected posterior rows when absolute cache paths change; that RDS metadata change does not constitute a chronology refit.

## Scientific decisions left with the author

The existing six-question register remains unchanged. Evidence supports retaining a fixed calibration SD as a clearly defined reference, while its cancellation from the era threshold limits what an alternative SD could change. The raw TraCE21k/V2.1 anchoring question remains a product-comparability decision; a matched modern anchor does not establish a valid correction across the Holocene. CHELSA precipitation continuity and unequal survey windows remain qualifications. Original MORA survey-date documentation and corrected CARI coordinates require external evidence. Own-origin versus common-origin geometry defines different scientific estimands; this audit verifies the existing calculation without selecting another one.

Additional decisions concern recoverable CRS age uncertainty, the probability interpretation of tephra/maximum-age controls, and whether Figure 3 should use equal endpoint membership. These are proposed for discussion; no method, threshold, coordinate, calibration membership, chronology, or geometric framework was replaced. Passing software checks does not resolve these scientific questions or validate a causal interpretation.
